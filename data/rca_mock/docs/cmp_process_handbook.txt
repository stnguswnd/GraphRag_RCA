CMP Process Handbook — Defect Signatures
Process Step: CMP

1. Donut defects from pad wear

Chemical mechanical polishing (CMP) can produce a Donut defect: a ring-shaped band
of failing die at mid radius. The dominant root cause is polishing pad wear. As the
pad wears unevenly over its lifetime, the material removal rate at the wafer edge
increases relative to the center. The over-polished annular zone manifests as the
donut-shaped defect band. Because the pad is a consumable, this is treated as a
Material type cause, and it is associated with the polish down-force (polish
pressure) running high in the affected zone.

2. Scratch defects from slurry contamination

A second, distinct CMP signature is the Scratch pattern — linear defects that cut
across the wafer surface. The usual cause is slurry particle contamination: when
slurry particles agglomerate, the hardened clumps drag across the wafer during
polishing and gouge linear scratches. This is a Material type cause tied to the
slurry flow parameter.

3. Confirming the causes

For both signatures, polish pressure and slurry flow telemetry should be reviewed.
Pad wear and slurry contamination are both material/consumable issues, so maintenance
history (pad change, slurry lot change) and alarm history are informative. The
normal-lot ratio on the same polisher serves as a negative control, and the yield
change point helps date the onset.
