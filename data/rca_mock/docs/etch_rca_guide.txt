Plasma Etch Root-Cause Analysis Guide
Process Step: ETCH

1. Edge-Ring defects and the focus ring

One of the most common edge-related failure signatures in plasma etch is the
Edge-Ring pattern, in which failing die form a continuous band along the wafer
periphery. Field experience shows that focus ring erosion in the etch chamber is
a leading root cause. As the focus ring physically wears down over many RF hours,
plasma confinement at the wafer edge degrades. This degradation typically forces
the tool to compensate with elevated RF power, and the combination produces a
characteristic edge ring defect pattern.

The mechanism is a two-step chain. First, focus ring erosion (a machine/consumable
issue) develops in the chamber. Second, the weakened edge confinement causes etch
rate non-uniformity that is concentrated along the outer ring of the wafer. It is
this etch rate non-uniformity that directly manifests as the Edge-Ring pattern, so
in causal terms the non-uniformity is the surface cause and focus ring erosion is
the deeper root cause behind it.

2. Confirming the cause

When an Edge-Ring signature appears, RF power telemetry for the suspect chamber
should be reviewed first, since elevated RF power is the primary tell. Maintenance
history is equally important: a focus ring that is overdue for replacement is
strong corroborating evidence. Alarm history around the affected time window may
also show plasma-instability events. Finally, the normal-lot ratio on the same
chamber should be checked as a negative control before concluding.
