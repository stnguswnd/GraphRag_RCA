Deposition Defect Catalog
Process Step: DEPO

1. Center defects from gas-flow imbalance

Center-clustered failures on the wafer map are frequently traced back to the
deposition step. When the process gas is not delivered uniformly, the film grows
thicker at the wafer center than at the edge. This center-thick film profile
manifests as a Center defect cluster at electrical test.

The immediate surface cause is gas flow imbalance during deposition: insufficient
precursor gas flow starves the outer radius while the center over-deposits. Because
the imbalance is a process-parameter deviation, it should be treated as a Parameter
type cause, and it is tied to the deposition gas flow parameter running low.

2. Upstream cause: mass flow controller drift

Gas flow imbalance is often not the deepest cause. A frequent upstream driver is
mass flow controller (MFC) drift in the deposition tool. As the MFC drifts out of
calibration, it skews the delivered gas ratios, which in turn produces the flow
imbalance described above. MFC drift is a machine issue and therefore also connects
to the deposition gas flow parameter reading low.

3. Confirming the cause

Deposition gas flow telemetry should be examined for the suspect chamber and time
window. Because MFC drift is a machine fault, maintenance and alarm history for the
tool are useful, and the normal-lot ratio provides a negative control. When the
Center signature is confirmed, the yield change point is worth locating to bound the
onset of the excursion.
